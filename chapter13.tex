\chapter{Management Review Simulation}
\label{chap:management_review}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

This chapter **simulates** the top-management review required by
ISO/IEC 27001:2022, Clause 9.3, and provides a critical reflection on the work
performed during the \ac{ISMS} project.  It is delivered in two
self-contained parts:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Management-Review Report} – documentation of the simulated
        meeting, including required inputs, decisions, and actions
        (Clauses 9.3.2 – 9.3.3);
  \item \textbf{Critical-Reflection Report} – individual and team
        reflections on project achievements, limitations, and improvement
        opportunities.
\end{enumerate}

%----------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Management-Review Report}
\label{sec:mgmt_review_report}

\subsection{General}

The simulated review was chaired by the \ac{CEO} on
\textbf{5 June 2025} with attendance from the full Executive Management
Team, the \ac{CISO}, and the directors of Engineering \& Grid Operations,
Supply Chain Management, Research \& Development, Customer
Services \& Billing, \ac{HR}, and Legal \& Administration.
The objective was to evaluate the continuing suitability, adequacy, and
effectiveness of the \ac{ISMS} and to identify opportunities for
improvement.

        \emph{Owner:} OT Security Engineer \quad \emph{Due:} 30 Nov 2025
  \item \textbf{A4 – Procure AI-driven phishing-awareness platform}  
        \emph{Owner:} CISO / Procurement \quad \emph{Due:} 30 Oct 2025
  \item \textbf{A5 – Update BCP contact lists and retest in next
        tabletop exercise}  
        \emph{Owner:} BCM Coordinator \quad \emph{Due:} 31 Jul 2025

\paragraph{Resource needs}

The Board approved an additional \textbf{€ 250 k} CAPEX for OT
zero-trust hardware and \textbf{€ 70 k} OPEX for the phishing-simulation
subscription.

\paragraph{Policy and objective changes}

No change to the Information-Security Policy was required.  
The OT anomaly-detection target was raised from 90 \% to
\textbf{95 \%} coverage by Q2 2026.

\paragraph{Risk acceptance}

Residual risk \emph{IT-201} (phishing) remains \textbf{Medium-High};
acceptance is deferred until the results of Action A4 are reviewed
(Q4 2025).

\paragraph{Effectiveness statement}

Management concluded that the \ac{ISMS} remains
\emph{effective and aligned} with organisational goals, contingent on the
timely completion of Actions A1–A5.

\section{Critical-Reflection Report}
\label{sec:critical_reflection}

\subsection{Methodological strengths}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Iterative risk loop} – Re-using the same
        $5\times5$ matrix across Activities 6, 12 and 13 provided a clear
        audit trail and simplified stakeholder communication.
  \item \textbf{Cross-department engagement} – Involving SCADA, supply-chain,
        and R\&D experts enhanced asset inventories and made recovery
        metrics more realistic.
  \item \textbf{Clause-mapped deliverables} – Anchoring every chapter to a
        specific ISO/IEC 27001 clause prevented scope creep and ensured
        audit-ready documents.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Challenges encountered}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{OT/IT convergence complexity} – Mapping Annex A controls
        to mixed OT assets demanded extensive interpretation and, at times,
        bespoke controls (e.g.\ secure-firmware signing).
  \item \textbf{Granularity versus confidentiality} – Privacy constraints
        limited access to incident details, forcing reliance on aggregated
        KPI data.
  \item \textbf{Resource availability} – Simulated exercises uncovered
        single-point-of-knowledge risks (HR-502) underestimated in the
        initial BIA.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Lessons learned}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Validate early, correct less} – Pilot tests (e.g.\ cloud
        logging) should run in parallel with policy drafting to avoid
        post-implementation NCs.
  \item \textbf{Visual dashboards drive buy-in} – Simple traffic-light
        KPIs resonated more with the Board than detailed spreadsheets.
  \item \textbf{Continuity plans must stay current} – Minor organisational
        changes (staff churn, new wind farm) quickly render contact trees
        and \ac{RTO}/\ac{RPO} assumptions obsolete.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Opportunities for further improvement}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Integrate \emph{MITRE ATT\&CK for ICS} to refine threat scenarios
        and better quantify OT-specific residual risks.
  \item Automate evidence intake (backup-verification logs, vulnerability
        scans) into the \ac{ISMS} repository to streamline future audits.
  \item Extend supplier-security clauses to mandate software bills of
        materials (SBOM) for all critical firmware and third-party code.
\end{itemize}
