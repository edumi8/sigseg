\chapter{Statement of Applicability}
\label{chap:soa}

This chapter maps each risk identified in the assessment to the relevant ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A controls and documents whether a control is \textit{included} or \textit{excluded}.  
For clarity, items are grouped into two categories:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Shared Assets} - Information system components and services that are shared across the organisation.
    \item \textbf{Departmental Assets} - Assets that belong to, or are primarily managed by, a specific department.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Shared-asset control focus.}
\emph{ERP-301} underpins finance, HR and supply-chain workflows; without the strengthened access controls in A.9 a single compromised credential could expose all corporate data.  
\emph{IT-201} demonstrates that social-engineering is still the quickest route around technical safeguards; awareness training (A.6.3.1) and periodic refreshers (A.7.2.2) directly lower attack success-rates.  
For \emph{IT-202}, tight network zoning (A.13.1.1) and correlated event logs (A.12.4.1) ensure any firewall mis-rule is quickly detected and isolated.  
Customer-records server \emph{CS-401} hinges on least-privilege (A.9.2.x); quarterly reviews eliminate dormant high-privilege accounts that could leak PII.  
\emph{HR-501} shares that privileged HR roles hold payroll and medical data; admin-session logging (A.12.4.3) provides the evidential trail to deter and detect abuse.  
Finally, \emph{HR-502} addresses business-continuity: cross-training and an up-to-date succession matrix (A.17.1.1, A.5.30) protect operations during pandemics or sudden departures.

\paragraph{Supply-chain control focus.}
The shared \texttt{ERP-301} instance also drives procurement, making it the single integration point with dozens of vendors.  
A breakdown in supplier diligence could embed malware into firmware deliveries; controls A.15.1.1 and A.15.2.1 introduce binding security clauses, assessment schedules and service-level triggers.  
Patch failures remain the prime cause of \emph{ERP} data corruption; adopting segregated test environments plus dual approval (A.12.1.2, A.14.2.2) minimises that risk.  
Finally, every minute of \emph{ERP} downtime stalls purchase orders and grid-maintenance spares, so hot-standby infrastructure (A.17.1.2) and geographic replication (A.17.2.1) are non-negotiable for supply-chain resilience.
